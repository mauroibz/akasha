Akasha is released under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3.0**
(see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)). For almost everyone, that is the only licence that
matters and nothing here applies to you.

## You do not need to read this if

- You run Akasha for yourself, your family, or your household.
- Your school, library, university, lab, or employer runs it internally for
  their own people.
- You fork it, modify it, and keep those changes to yourself.
- You publish your changes under the AGPL as well.

All of the above are free, permanently, and require no permission and no
contact. That is the point of the licence.

## What the AGPL asks in return

If you convey Akasha to others, or run a modified version as a network service
that other people use, you must make your complete corresponding source
available to those users under the AGPL. Section 13 of the licence is the
clause that extends this to network use.

In practice this means you cannot take Akasha, modify it, and offer it as a
closed-source hosted product.

## If the AGPL does not work for you

If you want to redistribute Akasha, or offer it as a service, **without** the
obligation to release your source under the AGPL, a separate commercial licence
is available. The copyright is held by a single author, so it can be granted
directly.

Open an issue titled `Commercial licence enquiry`, or contact the maintainer
through the address on the repository's GitHub profile. Describe what you want
to build; terms are negotiated case by case, and small or non-profit uses are
usually free.

## Contributions

Contributions are accepted under the AGPL, the same terms as the project. If a
contribution is substantial enough that dual-licensing it would require your
permission, that will be discussed in the pull request rather than assumed.
